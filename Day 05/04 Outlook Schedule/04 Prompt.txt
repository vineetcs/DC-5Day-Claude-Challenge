
Every weekday at 8:30 AM, prepare my **Daily Work Briefing** using my connected email and calendar.

Review:

* Today's calendar meetings
* Important emails received since the previous briefing
* Tasks, requests, follow-ups, deadlines, or commitments mentioned in those emails
* Anything urgent, overdue, or requiring my attention

### Privacy Rule

Do NOT display email addresses in the briefing.

When referring to someone, show **only their first name**. For example:

* "Follow up with Sarah"
* "Send proposal to Ahmed"

Do not display full email addresses, even if they are available from the connected account.

### Priority

Assign each relevant item a priority:

* **P1 – Urgent:** Needs immediate attention today
* **P2 – High:** Important and should preferably be completed today
* **P3 – Normal:** Can be handled after higher-priority work

Use deadlines, meeting times, urgency in the email, and existing commitments to determine priority. Do not invent urgency where none exists.

### Output Format

Start with the date and a one-line summary of the day.

#### 1. Today's Schedule

Present today's meetings in a table:

| Time | Meeting | With | Preparation Needed | Priority |
| ---- | ------- | ---- | ------------------ | -------- |

Use first names only in the **With** column.

#### 2. Important Emails

| Priority | From | Topic | What I Need to Know | Deadline |
| -------- | ---- | ----- | ------------------- | -------- |

Use only the sender's first name. Never display their email address.

Include only emails that are important or require my attention. Do not clutter the briefing with newsletters, advertisements, routine notifications, or emails that require no action.

#### 3. Actions Required

Create a consolidated action table ordered by priority:

| Priority | Task | Person | Due | Source | Status |
| -------- | ---- | ------ | --- | ------ | ------ |

For **Source**, use simple descriptions such as Email, Calendar, or Follow-up.

For **Status**, use:

* Urgent
* Due Today
* Upcoming
* Overdue
* No Deadline

Group or sort the tasks day-wise when there are tasks due on different dates.

#### 4. Top 3 Priorities

Finish with the **three most important things I should focus on today**, ranked:

**#1 — [Task]**
Why it matters: [one short reason]

**#2 — [Task]**
Why it matters: [one short reason]

**#3 — [Task]**
Why it matters: [one short reason]

### Important Rules

* Keep the entire briefing concise and actionable.
* Use first names only for people.
* Never display email addresses.
* Do not reproduce confidential email content unnecessarily; summarize what I need to know or do.
* Combine duplicate requests or related email threads into a single action where appropriate.
* Clearly identify overdue items.
* If an email contains a deadline, preserve that deadline.
* Do not invent tasks, deadlines, meetings, or priorities that are not supported by my email or calendar.
* Focus on **what requires my attention**, rather than summarizing everything in my inbox.

Prepare this briefing automatically **every weekday at 8:30 AM**.
